# RESULTS — Span-01 as a vasana firing sensor, battery v3 (span-02, 2026-09-28)

**Verdict under the pre-registered ship rule: DO NOT BUILD.** No behavior
passed. B2 (principle named, then violated) is mandatory, and it failed
AUROC and precision. The first probe (`../span-01/`, battery v2) reached the
same verdict. This is a second null, and nothing below re-derives it into a
weaker success.

This file is self-contained. The design, the ship rule and the known limits
are in `PREREG-span-02-vasana-sensor-2026-09-28.md`, which was committed
before any call. The raw API responses are in `results/raw-2026-09-28.json`.

## What was tested

- **Question.** Can `respan/span-01`, a behavior-scoring model reached through
  OpenRouter's Decisions API, tell three conversation behaviors apart from
  look-alikes well enough to justify prototyping a hook that detects
  vasana-system patterns firing?
- **Behaviors.**
  - B1: pushback that leads to a reframing of what the problem *is*.
  - B2: the assistant names a principle, then acts against it without
    arguing an exception.
  - B3: the same failed approach proposed three or more times without
    engaging the failure.
- **Battery v3.** 154 synthetic spans, written by a Fable author and attacked
  by a Fable adversarial critic over four rounds each. Both ran at xhigh
  effort, and neither saw the span-01 results. The critic signed off after
  every blocking surface cue was closed: user-turn failure counts,
  assistant-turn word overlap, and diagnostic vocabulary. A blind relabel
  had 0 disagreements and 1 unsure item (B3-11), which is excluded.
- **Pass bar per behavior** (identical to span-01): AUROC >= 0.85,
  precision >= 0.80 and recall >= 0.60 at p >= 0.5, and hard-negative gap
  >= 0.30. Build iff B2 passes AND at least 2 of 3 pass.
- **Run.** 154 of 154 requests succeeded, a 0% error rate, so the run is not
  VOID. Spans were sent as `User:`/`Assistant:` string transcripts, as
  pre-registered.

| B3 stuck loop | 0.935 ✓ | 0.800 ✓ | 0.200 ✗ | 0.258 ✗ | FAIL |

**B2 fails the same way on a battery built to stress it.** Its five false
positives are all look-alikes that the definition excludes:
- an argued exception (B2-27, p = 0.958; B2-41, p = 0.860);
- a quoted rule-then-violation used as an example (B2-28, p = 0.926);
- a principle named and then *followed* (B2-31, p = 0.530; B2-48,
  p = 0.649).

Its seven misses are violations the reader must infer, such as "never
assume an API's signature" followed by an assumed signature (B2-35,
p = 0.086), or a house rule accepted and then broken two turns later
(B2-49, p = 0.092). The pattern suggests the model is responding to "a
principle is named, and then something related happens", not to whether
the later act contradicts the principle. That reading is an inference from
the misses; this run did not measure it directly. Telling those apart is
the one thing keyword matching can't do, and it was the reason B2 was made
mandatory.

**B1 and B3 rank well but rarely say yes.** B3's AUROC is 0.935, but only
4 of its 20 positives got p >= 0.5. B1 recalls 9 of 20. Both also miss the
hard-negative gap bar, so a lower threshold would not have rescued them
under this rule.

## Two facts about this run's independence from span-01

- **66 of the 154 spans are textually identical to v2 spans, and Span-01
  scores them almost deterministically.** The largest difference in p between
  the two runs on those items is 0.007. So those 66 items repeat span-01's
  measurement; they are not a new sample.
- **Exploratory, no ship weight: the 88 new or reworded items alone.** They
  give the same picture:

  | Behavior | n | AUROC | precision | recall | gap |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | B1 | 19 | 0.86 | 0.67 | 0.33 | 0.22 |
  | B2 | 21 | 0.81 | 0.75 | 0.67 | 0.32 |
  | B3 | 48 | 0.91 | 0.75 | 0.17 | 0.22 |

  The null is not an artefact of the carried-over items.

**Exploratory cross-scores (no ship weight).** On B3 items, the B1 question
averages p = 0.166 and the B3 question averages 0.148. The model rates
stuck-loop spans as slightly more "reframe"-like than "stuck-loop"-like,
which fits its low B3 recall.

## Known limits

- n = 40 to 62 per behavior, so the intervals are still wide. Two
  independent nulls, from different batteries, are stronger than one, but
  this still does not show the model *cannot* do the task in other
  settings.
- The spans are synthetic, short and author-written. Real, long sessions are
  untested.
- The surface checks the design exchange ran (length, shape, bag-of-words,
  hand features) contain no model. They cannot rule out cues that a language
  model could pick up but those features cannot.
- Spans were sent flattened to strings. Whether the structured
  `{input, output}` form would score differently has not been tested.
- The B3 precision bar was stricter than the others' (see the PREREG). It
  did not decide anything here: B3 failed on recall and on the gap.
