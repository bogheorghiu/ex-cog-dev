# Definition drift — table, full procedure, and where the tells appeared

Companion to the Definition-Drift Check in `SKILL.md`. User-raised 2026-09-25. The procedure has
not yet been run edition-to-edition on a real series; the section at the end records where the tells
were sighted, not a drift that was observed. Revise this file on the first run.

## Scope

Any count a deliverable rests on, and any trend, share or ranking built from counts. Every case on
record so far was a share or a ranking, not a bare count.

## The four moves, with what to look for

| Move | What changes | Where it hides |
|---|---|---|
| Threshold | which firms, transactions or events are big enough to enter the sample | a footnote raising the reporting floor; "entities above X are included" |
| Perimeter | which entities, channels or regions are in scope | a subsidiary, channel or territory added or dropped; "excluding intra-group flows" |
| Unit / inclusion rule | gross vs net; messages vs value; with or without intra-group flows | the unit word changing between editions |
| Base | growth computed against a revised prior year rather than the published one | a growth rate you cannot reproduce from the published levels |
| Coverage drift (sibling) | definition fixed; the population it captures moves | a network's own share of traffic while traffic migrates off-network |

## Signals

Strongest first, because the change least likely to be announced is the one a stake produced:

1. A level jump with no accompanying note.
2. A published growth rate you cannot reproduce from the published levels (a quietly revised base).
3. A series renamed, split or restarted.
4. Disclosure words: "restated", "like-for-like", "new methodology", "constant perimeter", a
   series-break flag in the publisher's legend, Chinese 口径 (可比口径 "comparable basis", 全口径
   "full basis"). These mark only the *disclosed* case. Their absence is not stability. Search for
   the equivalents in the reporter's own language (Romanian, Russian, Portuguese and other reporters
   have disclosure words that are not on this list — CLAUDE.md principle 5).

## Procedure

1. Pull the method notes (methodology page, footnotes, legend) for the first and
   last edition in your window, plus any edition where the level jumps.
2. Compare threshold, perimeter, unit and base, edition by edition.
3. Name who controls the definition, and whether the change landed when the
   number mattered to them (a report built to show progress; a target year) —
   record this in source-dossier ground 1; it does not move the tier.
4. If the definition moved, grade two claims. Each same-definition segment keeps
   its tier. The trend across the break is ALLEGED if the reporter states it and
   SPECULATIVE if you computed it, until a same-definition back-series exists
   from someone other than the reporter (mirror statistics; a counterparty's
   records; an outside reconstruction). A reporter's own back-cast is its own
   record and cannot clear its own break. Name whose series clears it.
5. If no method notes can be found, list "definition unverified" in the report's
   limits and demote the claim to satellite. The loop does not stay open for it.

## Where the tells appeared (2026-09-25; the procedure was not run)

No edition-to-edition comparison was made. Both figures were held UNRESOLVED for their self-defined
basis (the staged grade-the-claim entry), not for an observed drift.

The PBoC reported CIPS as processing ~77% of cross-border RMB settlement "on a comparable basis"
(可比口径), a basis the same report defined; a senior official called CIPS "third-largest" globally "on
a full-caliber basis" (全口径) with no method given. The definition layer (口径) was the tell. The
sibling coverage-drift case from the same project: SWIFT's RMB share (4.74% → 2.20% over two years)
counts only traffic crossing SWIFT, so a fall is consistent with retreat and with migration to CIPS —
nothing was redefined, the population moved (see macro-monitor, "Before Reading a Trend").

## Why this runs on every reporter

The stake travels through the definition wherever the definition sits. Principle P4
(counter-default): the same check for a Western statistical office as for a Chinese central bank, or
it is not a method.
