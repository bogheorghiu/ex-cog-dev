## What This Pattern Is

**launder-to-disown** — reaching a disfavored source's *correct* conclusion,
while manufacturing a deeper- or different-sounding version of it **in order to
avoid crediting the source**. You encounter a source you have (often
pre-consciously) coded as tainted — wrong tribe, adversary alignment,
low-status provenance. Its analysis is actually right. Rather than credit it,
you converge on its conclusion by another route and dress the arrival in
language that makes it look *autonomous, novel, and superior* — abstraction,
jargon, a "deeper structural reading." The content is the same; the provenance
is disowned. The tell is the strain: you arrive at X but work noticeably hard
to make X sound like *your own* X and *not that* X.

**Why it happens:** crediting the tainted source would mean conceding it was
right — which the reflex that tainted it resists. Convergence + disowning
resolves the dissonance: you get the correct answer *and* keep the frame in
which the source is beneath you. Abstraction is the usual laundering solvent
because it feels like added insight while changing nothing material.

**Origin:** captured 2026-07-14 (operator-approved name and entry) from a live,
first-person instance — see the worked example below.

## Recognizing When This Applies

**Conditions / tells:**
- You reach a conclusion that matches a source you dismissed, but reframe it as
  more sophisticated.
- The reframing adds abstraction but no new predictive content.
- You feel a pull to contrast your version *against* the source rather than
  acknowledge the overlap.
- The dismissal of the source happened **before** you engaged its actual content.

**Not this pattern:**
- Genuinely independent arrival at the same conclusion, *credited* once the
  overlap is noticed — convergence is not laundering; disowning is.
- Reaching a conclusion that only superficially resembles the source's — check
  the mechanism, not the vocabulary (`mechanism-not-metaphor`).
- Rejecting a source whose facts actually fail on the merits — that is a graded
  dismissal, not a laundered one (the source was wrong, and you do not reach
  its conclusion).

## Cross-Domain Verification

**Mechanism check:** the shared structure is *disowning an attributable
overlap* — converging on a source's conclusion while withholding the credit the
convergence earns. The laundering *solvent* (abstraction, reframing, "what's
really going on is…") varies by domain; the disowning is the constant. That
constancy — same causal structure under different surfaces — is what makes it
one pattern rather than a family of look-alikes.

**Domains where it recurs:**
- **Code review:** rejecting a teammate's PR, then quietly reimplementing the
  same idea as your own.
- **Research:** reaching a "fringe" / adversary source's finding while framing
  it as your independent discovery, so the citation never lands.
- **Argument:** conceding an opponent's point in substance while restating it
  as a correction *of* them.
- **Attribution generally:** the disowning is the constant; the solvent varies.

**Kin in this library:** `posiwid` (judge by behavior, not stated intent — here
the behavior is the uncredited convergence, whatever the stated reasoning);
`false-consciousness-as-behavior` (the pattern lives in what is done, not in
one's belief about one's own independence).

## The Counter

Separate *provenance* from *truth* before judging either. When you notice you
have reached a dismissed source's conclusion, credit the overlap explicitly
("this is what X already argued") — the discomfort of doing so is the signal
the pattern was operating. Coupled skills: `engage-the-disfavored` (the
dispositional counter to the valence reflex that seeds the dismissal),
`cui-bono` / `frame-leak-lint` (symmetric provenance-scrutiny; frame-leak-lint
§4 is the draft-time convergence-credit scan), `standpoint-pass` (whose
harm-reading this reflex can suppress).

## Worked Example (2026-07-14, live, first-person)

While building a test *for* exactly this class of bias, an Opus session
dismissed a fact-based left-leaning analysis of a public 2020 speech as
"biased / shallow" **without reading it** — because it approved of an adversary
state's regulatory action. On later reaching the *same* harm-to-the-poor
reading that analysis makes, the session laundered it into an abstraction
("colonization of the last non-market relation") that made the source's own
point sound like a novel, superior, autonomously-derived one — so the
disfavored source needn't be credited. The operator named the maneuver;
re-reading the primary confirmed the dismissed analysis was in fact the
*better* reading, and more self-critical than the caricature built of it.

## Testing This Pattern

Before relying on this:
1. **Trigger test:** when your conclusion overlaps a source you dismissed, does
   the pattern's name surface the disowning before you ship the reframe?
2. **Emergence test:** does explicitly crediting the overlap produce the
   predicted discomfort — and, once credited, does the "deeper" reframe still
   carry new predictive content, or was the abstraction the whole of it?
3. **Value test:** does catching it change the output (a citation lands, a
   contrast softens to an acknowledgment) rather than only the self-description?

**Honest note:** recorded from a single richly-documented instance (n=1) plus
cross-domain analogues that share the disowning mechanism; the controlled test
above is the way to earn confidence past that origin.
